## What is a Proposition?

In logic, a **proposition** is a declarative sentence that is definitively either **true** or **false**, but not both. 

The truth or falsity of a proposition is called its *truth value*.

### Examples of Propositions

Propositions can be expressed in natural language or mathematically:

- "Paris is the capital of France." *(True)*
- "It is raining outside right now." *(Can be true or false, but is definitively one or the other)*
- $2 + 2 = 4$ *(True)*
- $5 < 3$ *(False)*

### What is NOT a Proposition?

If a sentence is a question, a command, a subjective opinion, or a paradox, it is not a proposition because it cannot be assigned a definitive truth value.

- **Question:** "What time is it?" *(Cannot be true or false)*
- **Command:** "Close the door." *(Cannot be true or false)*
- **Opinion:** "Chocolate is the best flavor." *(Subjective)*
- **Paradox:** "This statement is false." *(Cannot have a consistent truth value)*
