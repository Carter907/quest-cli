## What is Logical OR?

In propositional logic, **Logical OR** (also called **disjunction**) is an operation that connects two propositions.

The disjunction of two propositions $P$ and $Q$ is denoted as:

$$ P \lor Q $$

(Read as *"P or Q"*).

### Truth Value Rule

Standard propositional logic uses the **Inclusive OR**, meaning a logical disjunction $P \lor Q$ is **true** if $P$ is true, if $Q$ is true, or if **both** are true. It is only false when both $P$ and $Q$ are false.

### Examples

- **Natural Language:**
  - Let $P$ = "I will wear a hat."
  - Let $Q$ = "I will wear sunglasses."
  - $P \lor Q$ = "I will wear a hat or I will wear sunglasses." *(True if you wear a hat, sunglasses, or both!)*

- **Mathematical:**
  - $(3 < 5) \lor (2 + 2 = 5)$ is **True** because the first component is true.
  - $(5 < 3) \lor (2 + 2 = 5)$ is **False** because both components are false.
