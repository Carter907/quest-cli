## What is Negation?

In logic, **Negation** is an operation that takes a single proposition and flips its truth value. If a proposition is true, its negation is false. If it is false, its negation is true.

The negation of a proposition $P$ is commonly denoted as:

$$ \neg P $$

(Read as *"Not P"*).

### Examples

- **Natural Language:**
  - Let $P$ = "The sky is blue." *(True)*
  - $\neg P$ = "The sky is not blue." *(False)*

- **Mathematical:**
  - Let $P$ = $5 < 3$ *(False)*
  - $\neg P$ = $\neg(5 < 3)$ or $5 \ge 3$ *(True)*

### Double Negation

A common and useful property is **Double Negation**. If you negate a proposition twice, you return to its original truth value. 

For example, "It is *not* true that the sky is *not* blue" simply means "The sky is blue."
