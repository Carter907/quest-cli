## The Zero Conditional

The **zero conditional** is used to express general truths, scientific facts, or situations that are always real and possible. It describes a condition that absolutely always yields the same result.

### Structure

Both clauses in the zero conditional use the **present simple** tense.

- **If Clause:** Present Simple
- **Main Clause:** Present Simple

### Examples

- "If you heat ice, it melts."
- "If people do not eat, they get hungry."
- "Wood floats if you drop it in water."

### Meaning

In the zero conditional, the word "if" can almost always be replaced with "when" without changing the meaning of the sentence, because the outcome is guaranteed. 

*(e.g., "When you heat ice, it melts.")*
