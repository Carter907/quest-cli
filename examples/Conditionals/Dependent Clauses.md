## What is a Dependent Clause?

A **dependent clause** (also known as a subordinate clause) is a clause that contains a subject and a verb but **does not** express a complete thought. As a result, it cannot stand alone as a valid sentence.

### Characteristics

1. **Has a subject and a verb:** It follows the basic structure of a clause.
2. **Begins with a subordinate word:** It usually starts with a subordinating conjunction (e.g., *because*, *although*, *if*, *when*) or a relative pronoun (e.g., *which*, *who*).
3. **Incomplete thought:** When read aloud on its own, it leaves the listener expecting more information to resolve the sentence.

### Examples

- "...because it was raining heavily"
- "...although he tried his best"
- "...if you need any help"

If you were to walk up to someone and say "Although he tried his best," they would be confused and wait for you to finish your thought.

### Role in Sentences

Dependent clauses exist to add extra context, conditions, or descriptive information to an **independent clause**. They must be attached to an independent clause to form a complete, grammatically correct sentence.
