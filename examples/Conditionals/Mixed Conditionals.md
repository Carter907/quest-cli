## Mixed Conditionals

Sometimes, the time frames of the condition and the result do not match up. **Mixed conditionals** combine elements of the Second and Third conditionals to express a past condition with a present result, or a present condition with a past result.

### 1. Past Condition -> Present Result

This structure imagines how a change in a past event would alter the present reality. It mixes a Third Conditional if-clause with a Second Conditional main clause.

- **Structure:** If + Past Perfect, *would* + Base Verb
- **Example:** "If I had taken that job in Paris (past action), I would be fluent in French now (present result)."

### 2. Present/General Condition -> Past Result

This structure imagines how a general, ongoing state (which is currently untrue) would have affected a specific event in the past. It mixes a Second Conditional if-clause with a Third Conditional main clause.

- **Structure:** If + Past Simple, *would have* + Past Participle
- **Example:** "If I were taller (ongoing state), I would have been picked for the basketball team last year (past result)."

### Meaning

Mixed conditionals highlight the interconnectedness of time, allowing us to smoothly discuss how our past choices affect our present, and how our permanent traits influenced our past.
