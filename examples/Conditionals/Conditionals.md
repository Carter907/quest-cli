## What is a Conditional Sentence?

A **conditional sentence** is a sentence that expresses a condition and the resulting consequence of that condition. It describes a cause-and-effect relationship, indicating that an action or event (the result) relies on something else happening first (the condition).

### Structure

Conditionals are composed of two distinct parts:
1. **The Condition:** Expressed in the **If Clause** (a dependent clause).
2. **The Result:** Expressed in the **Main Clause** (an independent clause).

These clauses can be placed in any order without changing the meaning of the sentence.

- **[If Clause], + [Main Clause]**
  - *If you mix blue and yellow, you get green.* (Note the comma when the if-clause comes first).
- **[Main Clause] + [If Clause]**
  - *You get green if you mix blue and yellow.* (No comma needed when the main clause comes first).

### Types of Conditionals

In English grammar, conditional sentences are divided into different types (Zero, First, Second, Third, and Mixed) based on the likelihood of the condition happening and the time frame (past, present, or future) being discussed.
