## The Third Conditional

The **third conditional** is used to talk about impossible, unreal situations in the **past**. It describes a condition in the past that did not happen, and imagines what the result would have been if it had.

### Structure

Because the condition is entirely in the past, it uses the past perfect tense. The result uses a perfect conditional structure.

- **If Clause:** Past Perfect (*had* + past participle)
- **Main Clause:** *Would have* + Past Participle

### Examples

- "If I had left earlier, I would have caught the train." *(Reality: I didn't leave early, so I missed the train.)*
- "If she had studied harder, she would have passed the test."
- "We wouldn't have gotten lost if you had brought the map."

### Meaning

The third conditional is often used to express regret, relief, or criticism about past events that cannot be changed. It is the language of hindsight.
