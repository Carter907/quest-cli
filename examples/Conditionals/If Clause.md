## The If Clause

In the context of conditional sentences, the **if-clause** is a specific type of dependent clause. Its primary function is to introduce a **condition**—a hypothetical or real scenario that must occur for a certain result to happen.

### Characteristics

- It usually starts with the subordinating conjunction **"if"** (though sometimes words like *when*, *unless*, or *provided that* are used).
- Because it is a dependent clause, it cannot stand alone. It must be paired with a main clause to complete the thought.

### Examples

- **"If it rains tomorrow..."** (Condition)
- **"If I had enough money..."** (Hypothetical condition)
- **"Unless you leave now..."** (Negative condition)

### Purpose

The if-clause sets the stage. It establishes the premise or the "cause" in a cause-and-effect relationship, dictating the circumstances under which the remainder of the sentence holds true.
