## The Main Clause

In a conditional sentence, the **main clause** is an independent clause that describes the **result** or consequence of the condition set out in the if-clause. 

### Characteristics

- Because it is an independent clause, it contains a subject and a verb and can technically stand on its own as a complete sentence.
- In conditionals, the main clause often utilizes modal verbs (like *will*, *would*, *can*, or *could*) to indicate the certainty or possibility of the result.

### Examples

- "...we will stay indoors." (Definite future result)
- "...I would buy a new car." (Hypothetical result)
- "...water boils." (Factual result)

### Interaction with the If-Clause

The main clause represents the "effect" in the cause-and-effect relationship. It tells us what happens *if* the condition is met. While the main clause can stand alone grammatically, its meaning in the context of the sentence is entirely dependent on the premise established by the if-clause.
