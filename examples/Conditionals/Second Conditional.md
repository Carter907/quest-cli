## The Second Conditional

The **second conditional** is used to imagine unreal, unlikely, or hypothetical situations in the present or future, and their imagined results.

### Structure

The condition is framed in the past simple (to show distance from reality), and the result uses the modal verb *would*.

- **If Clause:** Past Simple
- **Main Clause:** *Would* + Base Verb

*(Note: When using the verb "to be" in the if-clause, it is grammatically traditional to use "were" instead of "was" for all subjects, e.g., "If I were you...")*

### Examples

- "If I won the lottery, I would buy a mansion."
- "If I were the president, I would lower taxes."
- "He would travel the world if he had more free time."

### Meaning

The second conditional describes daydreams, wishes, or highly improbable scenarios. The condition is either impossible right now or extremely unlikely to happen in the future.
